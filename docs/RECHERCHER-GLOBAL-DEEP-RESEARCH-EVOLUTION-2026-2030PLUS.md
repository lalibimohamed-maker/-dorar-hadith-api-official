# Rechercher — Global Deep Research / Worldwide Knowledge Intelligence

Status: strategic master roadmap and implementation contract.

This document extends the V5–V20 roadmap and adds the missing knowledge-intelligence domains identified through the worldwide research pass. It is a roadmap and architecture contract, not a claim that future versions are already implemented.

## North-star

Rechercher evolves from a protected source-acquisition and knowledge platform into a source-grounded global research, learning, and knowledge-intelligence ecosystem:

WORLD SOURCES → DISCOVERY → IDENTITY → PROVENANCE → RIGHTS → VERSION/EDITION/MANUSCRIPT → TEXT/IMAGE/AUDIO/VIDEO → KNOWLEDGE → EVIDENCE → SEARCH → LEARNING → RESEARCH → MASTERY → CONTINUOUS EVOLUTION

The system must always be able to distinguish:
- what the source directly states;
- what is derived from multiple sources;
- what is scholarly interpretation;
- what is an AI-generated synthesis;
- what remains uncertain or unknown.

## Expanded knowledge domains — do not omit these

The knowledge layer must cover more than books and a generic graph. The following domains are first-class architectural concerns:

1. Bibliographic identity and entity resolution: person, organization, work, manifestation, edition, volume, copy, manuscript, scan, file and passage.
2. Provenance and chain-of-custody: acquisition event, transformation event, OCR engine/version, normalization, annotation, reviewer and hash lineage.
3. Rights and licensing intelligence: copyright status, permission, license, waqf restrictions, access restrictions, territorial constraints and publication/download permissions.
4. Digital preservation: fixity, checksums, format identification, migration history, redundant storage, archival snapshots and disaster recovery.
5. Manuscript and computational philology: foliation, scribal hands where documented, variant readings, collation, witness relationships, transcription confidence and editorial intervention.
6. Text intelligence: OCR, layout preservation, segmentation, passage identity, quotations, named entities, terminology, citations and cross-document alignment.
7. Multimodal intelligence: image, text, audio, video, speech, maps, diagrams and synchronized annotations.
8. Temporal intelligence: dates, eras, event sequences, version timelines, historical claims and time-dependent relationships.
9. Geospatial intelligence: people, places, routes, institutions, historical geography and map-linked evidence where sources support it.
10. Epistemic intelligence: claim type, evidence strength, uncertainty, attribution, direct-vs-derived status, authenticity-vs-existence distinction and unresolved questions.
11. Scholarly disagreement intelligence: madhhab/school, scholar, methodological tradition, alternative interpretations, counter-evidence and review state.
12. Citation intelligence: citation graph, quotation lineage, source-to-source references, edition-specific citations and citation stability.
13. Ontology and semantic interoperability: controlled vocabularies, persistent identifiers, mappings, validation constraints and versioned schemas.
14. Research-method intelligence: search strategy, inclusion/exclusion criteria, evidence grading, reproducibility, audit trails and limitations.
15. Educational intelligence: learner state, prerequisites, misconceptions, retrieval, spacing, interleaving, transfer, feedback, metacognition and mastery.
16. Human factors and accessibility: cognitive load, screen readers, keyboard access, dyslexia-friendly presentation, captions, multilingual UI and low-bandwidth/offline use.
17. Privacy and learner sovereignty: data minimization, local-first memory, export/delete, consent, retention controls and separation of private learner data from public knowledge.
18. Security and integrity intelligence: supply-chain protection, content integrity, provenance tamper detection, workflow security and safe failure isolation.
19. Quality and evaluation intelligence: retrieval quality, evidence coverage, OCR quality, learning outcomes, calibration, false-positive/false-negative analysis and regression testing.
20. Sustainability and resilience: free/open-source-first operation, reproducible deployments, resource-aware processing, queue resilience, backups and graceful degradation.
21. Community and governance: scholar review, contributor roles, moderation, transparent policies, appeal/revision paths and provenance of changes.
22. Knowledge gaps and negative evidence: explicit recording of not-found results, unresolved identity, missing editions, inaccessible sources and uncertainty; absence must never be silently promoted to non-existence.
23. Discovery intelligence: source registries, multilingual web discovery, institutional catalogs, scholarly graphs, archives, repositories and structured-data endpoints.
24. Interoperability: IIIF, persistent identifiers, JSON-LD/RDF where useful, validation constraints, portable manifests and standards-based exchange.
25. Observability: progress, source attempts, acquisition state, processing state, errors, retries, provenance and measurable completion without silently discarding failures.

## Global Deep Research loop

DISCOVER → IDENTIFY → DEDUPLICATE → VERIFY SOURCE → CHECK RIGHTS → ACQUIRE/REFERENCE → VALIDATE → EXTRACT → ALIGN → GRAPH → EVIDENCE → CONTRADICTION CHECK → SYNTHESIZE → HUMAN REVIEW → PUBLISH/EXPORT IF ALLOWED → MEASURE GAPS → RESEARCH AGAIN.

A failed source is recorded as a failed attempt, not silently discarded. A missing source is a knowledge-gap signal, not proof that the source does not exist.

## Golden rule

Rechercher does not become “AI that knows everything.”

It becomes intelligence that knows:
- where knowledge is;
- how knowledge objects relate;
- what the original source says;
- what edition/page/passage supports it;
- what rights apply;
- what evidence exists;
- how strong or uncertain the evidence is;
- where scholars disagree;
- what remains unknown;
- and how a human can reach the original source.

## Non-negotiable architecture boundaries

- Acquisition and persistence remain independent of learning/research intelligence.
- Original PDFs remain immutable after validated retention.
- Canonical Quran Arabic remains immutable.
- Rights and provenance are never bypassed by AI or research agents.
- AI is an assistant, not a mufti and not the final authority on religious interpretation.
- AI-generated educational/research artifacts are labeled until reviewed where authority matters.
- Scholarly disagreement remains visible and structured.
- Free/open-source-first remains the default.
- Every new capability requires tests, security review, provenance/rights validation, measurable evaluation, failure isolation and rollback/fallback.
- No future version is considered implemented merely because it exists in this roadmap.
